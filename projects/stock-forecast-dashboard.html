One week ahead

Bayesian local-level model

A hidden Markov model with two states and a local-level dynamic linear model in each of them, fit by Gibbs sampler on log prices. Both states are complete DLMs; the volatile one's variances are a common multiple of the calm one's, so a regime is a stretch where everything — the level's drift and the noise around it — is that many times larger. The chain decides which regime each day belongs to. The blue line is the fitted level against the actual close, the strip underneath is what is left over, and the fan is the 95 percent posterior predictive interval seven trading days out — a mixture over regime paths, since the chain can switch during the week.

What this is and is not The prices are the sample dataset bundled with the project for offline demos and tests, not a live market feed, and the forecasts were fit once when this page was built. That sample is generated as a constant-volatility random walk — one volatility per ticker, held for the whole two years — so there are no volatility regimes in it to find, and the two-state model correctly does not find any: the smoothed probability of the volatile state comes out flat, near the mixture weight, on every day of every ticker. That is why no stretch of the chart is shaded. The same model separates a genuinely turbulent stretch from a calm one when there is one to separate, which the project's test suite checks against data with a known regime change. A local-level model says the best guess for next week is roughly where the price is now, in either regime; what the two states buy is the width around that guess. A ticker sitting in the volatile regime today carries a wider fan than one in the calm regime, and because the chain can switch during the week the interval is a mixture over regime paths rather than one Gaussian. That is why the fan matters more here than the line down its middle, and why the ranking of expected returns is close to noise while the interval is not. Nothing here is investment advice.
