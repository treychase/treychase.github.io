One week ahead

Bayesian local-level model

A local-level dynamic linear model, fit by Gibbs sampler on log prices, run across a universe of stocks. For each one it returns not a number but a distribution: the posterior predictive price five trading days out. The shaded fan is the 95 percent credible interval, and it widens with the horizon because a random walk five days ahead is less certain than one day.

What this is and is not The prices are the sample dataset bundled with the project for offline demos and tests, not a live market feed, and the forecasts were fit once when this page was built. A local-level model says the best guess for next week is roughly where the price is now; what it is actually good for is the width of the interval around that, which is why the fan matters more here than the line down its middle. The projected movers make that concrete: across the whole universe the expected one-week returns span a few hundredths of a percent, while the 95 percent interval runs to about a sixth of the price. The ranking is noise; the interval is the answer. Nothing here is investment advice.
