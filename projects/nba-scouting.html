How the two metrics are built The pulls behind this page carry no box score, so one is reconstructed. Points and minutes come from the possessions pull, field goals from the shot chart, free throws from the difference between the two, and rebounds, assists, steals, blocks and rim defence from the tracking pulls. Summed over the league that lands on the season's real per‑team‑game rates, which is the check that nobody is being dropped or double counted. Turnovers are the one estimate: the pulls track them only on drives and on paint, post and elbow touches, so the rest are spread across each player's untracked touches at one league rate.

Every box event is then priced in points against what a possession is worth. Scoring needs no price — it is what a player scored above what the league would have scored on his own shot volume — and the rest are estimates kept in one table rather than buried in a formula. One is worth pulling out: the tracking pull credits a shot at the rim to whoever was nearest, and being nearest is an assignment rather than a choice. A guard is the closest defender at the rim mostly when he has already been beaten, and the league shows it — defenders facing the fewest rim attempts allow 72%, those facing the most allow 60%. So the baseline moves with volume, and a player is measured against what the league allows at his own rim volume.

Box plus/minus is those points per 100 possessions, centred so the league averages zero. Win shares read the same events through Dean Oliver's framework instead, which is why both are here rather than one being a rescaling of the other. Average WS/48 is 0.100, which is arithmetic and not a choice: win shares sum to wins, teams average .500, so a player who plays a fifth of his team's minutes earns a fifth of 41 wins. There is no opponent data in these pulls, so every team is treated as league‑average defensively — a player is credited for the stops he makes, not for the defence around him.

The archetypes

How the archetypes are built K‑means over fifteen tracking features in three families: field goal frequency and type (catch‑and‑shoot attempts and threes, pull‑up attempts and threes, drives, and attempts from the paint, the post and the elbow), passing (passes made, potential assists, assists per pass), and rebounding, steals and blocks. Deliberately no touch counts — a touch says a player had the ball, not what he did with it, so clustering on touches groups everyone who gets fed regardless of whether they shoot, pass or draw a foul. Everything is a per‑36 rate, so the clustering finds roles instead of rediscovering the minutes rotation.

The number of clusters comes off the silhouette score rather than a preference, subject to one rule: no archetype may hold more than a quarter of the league or fewer than 3% of it. Unconstrained, the silhouette prefers four clusters, one of which is half of everybody — true about the data, and useless to scout with. Each cluster is named by matching its centre against prototype weight vectors, and one defined only by what its players don't do is called a low‑usage role player rather than borrowing a name it has not earned. Players under 250 minutes are left out: their rate stats are mostly noise and would drag the cluster centres around.
