What the session cost

Unsupervised activity states · strain from movement alone

Three inertial units and a chest strap, sampled at 100 Hz through a twelve-activity protocol. Every ten-second window is scored for movement intensity, cadence, posture and cardiac response, and then clustered — with no access to the activity labels — into three states. Two of them are easy: the body is working, or it is still. The third is the one the labels cannot express. Sitting down before a run and sitting down after one are the same posture, the same near-zero acceleration and the same recorded activity; what separates them is a heart rate still paying off the bout. Recovery is a state you can only see in the biometrics.

The strain number underneath is Banister's TRIMP, which weights every minute by heart-rate reserve under an exponential and so treats a minute near maximum as worth far more than three easy ones. The model then predicts it from the accelerometers and the person's own body — never from heart rate — because that is the channel that goes missing when the strap is in a drawer. Scores are leave-one-subject-out: every number below was produced for a person the model had never seen.
